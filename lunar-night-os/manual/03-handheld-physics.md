# Handheld physics

The phone is light. Light things shake. Every hold below adds mass, adds contact points, or takes your breathing out of the frame.

## Holds for the kitchen

**LAW: three points of contact beat any stabilizer.**

| Hold | How | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Claw & Sternum Lock | Both hands claw the phone, thumbs behind, elbows pinned to your sternum | Walk-ups to the pass, slow pushes |
| Ninja Walk | Knees soft, heel-to-toe, hips carry the move, not the arms | Following a plate across the line |
| Towel Slider | Phone on a folded towel, slide it along clean steel | Low dolly past mise, sear close-ups |
| Apron-Loop Tension | Strap or apron loop pulled taut against the phone | 5x telephoto, where every tremor is magnified |
| Counter Brace | Wrists or phone edge resting on the counter | Static 1x and macro inserts |

> [!TIP] Breathe out, then roll
> Exhale, hold empty for the take, then start recording. Your chest stops moving when your lungs are empty.

## Night is braced, never handheld

**LAW: night reels are dead-still.**

Car roof, post, wall, or the Osmo locked as a stand. A 360° shutter makes every tremor smear, so the only motion allowed in a night shot is light moving through the frame.

## Stabilization modes

**LAW: choose the mode for the move, not out of habit.**

- **Standard**: walking and general handheld.
- **Locked**: static framing; it crops in and holds the horizon.
- **Active**: running or bigger moves; heavier crop.
- **Cinematic Pan**: slow, smooth pans; it dampens fast corrections.
- In Blackmagic Camera, stabilization **off** when the phone is braced.

> [!VERIFY] Mode names
> Open Pixel Camera › Video › settings and confirm the four stabilization names on your build. Label varies by version.

## Moves

**LAW: one move per shot.**

Push, pull, orbit, tilt reveal, slider, whip. A whip pan ends on a hard stop so it can cut against the matching whip in the next clip (see Transitions). Pushes on the bite, the money shot and the verdict: 8–15%, and 100%→108% over 3 s if you do it in CapCut instead.

## Shutter and blur

At 180° (1/48 at 24 fps, 1/60 at 30) motion blur looks natural. At 360° (1/24, 1/30) highlights streak: right for night car passes, wrong for knife work. Under 60 Hz LEDs, stay on multiples of 1/120 s or the lights band.

> [!RECALL] What is the hold for 5x telephoto? :: Apron-Loop Tension, a taut strap against the phone
> [!RECALL] When is handheld allowed at night? :: Never; braced on a car roof, post, wall or the Osmo
> [!RECALL] Which stabilization in Blackmagic when braced? :: Off
> [!RECALL] How many moves per shot? :: One
> [!RECALL] Flicker-safe shutters under 60 Hz LEDs? :: Multiples of 1/120 s
> [!RECALL] When do you breathe? :: Exhale, hold empty, then roll
