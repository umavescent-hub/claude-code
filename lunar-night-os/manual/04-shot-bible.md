# Shot bible

Every shot Kubrick knows, in one place: the 64 cards from the Shot Bible page, the eight Marketplace shots from your God Mode guide, and the night-exterior setups from the runbook, grouped by format. The numbers are the numbers the page uses. On the Shot Bible page a shot becomes a ticket on the rail: queue it, fire it, put it on the pass, or 86 it.

## How to read a row

**Law: Pick the lens, then roll. Tap the brightest part of the food and Lock before every take.**

| Column | What it holds |
|---|---|
| Shot | The card's id and name. A † means the card carries a device check; see the callout below. |
| Lens | 0.5x, 1x, 2x, 5x or the front camera. |
| Move | The camera vector, then the hold that makes it. |
| Length | What you keep. Roll longer and cut later. |
| Capture | Frame-rate code, ISO, brightness, white balance, stabilization. The shutter follows the code. |
| Purpose | The story role: hook, beat, breath, payoff or transition. |
| Pairs with | The cards it is cut next to, or shot on the same rig. |

| Lens | Camera | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5x, 12 mm | 48 MP ultrawide, ƒ/1.7 | Macro Focus engages at 3–4 cm and focuses to 2 cm. No OIS: brace it, keep it out of the dark, never on a night reel. |
| 1x, 24 mm | 50 MP main, ƒ/1.68, OIS | The biggest sensor and the best low light: the default for anything dark. |
| 2x, 48 mm | Centre crop of the main | Same glass, flatter perspective, so round plates stay round. |
| 5x, 113 mm | 48 MP periscope, ƒ/2.8, OIS | Needs light and distance. In auto it swaps to a main-camera crop closer than about 57 cm, and may in dim light: lock the lens. |

| Code | Shutter | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 4K24 | 1/48, or 1/60 if LEDs band | The film look. US mains pulse 120 times a second; 1/60 spans two pulses, 1/48 does not. |
| 4K30 | 1/60 | Flicker-safe under US mains light. |
| 4K60 | 1/120 | Slows cleanly to 0.4x on a 24 fps timeline. Needs about a stop and a third more light. |
| SLO120 | 1/240 | Slow Motion mode, 1080p, the 1/4x setting. Test for LED flicker first. |
| SLO240 | 1/480, or as fast as light allows | Slow Motion at 1/8x, 1080p. It wants daylight. |

Stabilization: **Standard** for handheld, **Locked** for 2x and 5x statics, **Active** only as a fallback (1080p only), **Cinematic Pan** for one slow pan in one direction.

The holds, in one line each:

- **Claw & Sternum Lock:** both hands claw the edges, elbows pinned, phone pulled to the sternum; move from the torso. Orbits, pans, whips, rises.
- **Ninja Walk:** soft knees, hips low, heel-to-toe steps, phone at chest or hip. Follows.
- **Towel Slider:** a folded bar towel on clean steel. Telephoto rests and dead-straight slides.
- **Apron-Loop Tension:** apron ties round the wrists, phone pushed out until the neck loop is taut. Drifts over the stove.
- **Counter Brace:** the phone's edge or the heel of your hand pressed onto the counter. Low statics and macros.
- **Elbow Tripod:** both elbows planted, forearms up, breathe out. 2x and 5x statics, anything from a seat.
- **Low Pass Drag:** a low claw with knuckles on the steel, dragged at an even pace. Lens-height glides.
- **Overhead Bridge:** a wire rack across two equal stacks of hotel pans, lens down. Overheads, grids, match cuts.
- **Napkin Cradle:** a cloth napkin folded into a wedge, the lens over the fold. Discreet table shots.
- **Deli-Quart Prop:** the phone leant on a full quart container, a towel under the edge for tilt. Solo shots and monologues.
- **Doorframe Lean:** shoulder and hip into a frame or post, feet wide. Long standing statics.

Capture rules that hold for every row: shutter at double the frame rate, except 1/60 at 24 fps under LEDs; ISO as low as the light allows, 800–1600 the ceiling in a dark dining room; brightness pulled down 0.3–0.7 so glazes and steel keep their highlights; white balance set for the room, not the food: candlelight 2800–3200K, LED kitchen 3800–4200K, golden-hour window 4800K, daylight prep 5400–5600K, night exteriors about 3200K. On the Pro XL the bottom mic sits beside the USB-C port; keep your palm off it, or turn the port to the left. Wipe the lens before every close shot.

> [!VERIFY] The device checks behind the † marks
> Sources disagree on whether the stock Pixel Camera lets you set shutter and ISO in Video: if yours does not, treat those numbers as targets and steer with light, Lock and brightness. Manual lens selection is documented for photos; check in `Settings › Pro › Lens Selection › Manual` that it also holds the 5x in Video. Locked at 4K60 is not documented; if it is not offered, use Standard with a brace. Slow Motion records 1080p: test one clip in CapCut before a big night, and roll a test under LEDs for flicker. Optimize FPS can drop 60 to 30 in dim light: turn it off before any 4K60 shot in a dark room. If 5x footage stutters, turn video stabilization off for the take; the telephoto's OIS still works. Cinematic Pan is listed for 4K and 1080p; confirm it offers 24 fps.

## Lunar Night Reel

**Law: One coloured light on one plain building, dead still, 30–75 minutes after sunset. No people.**

Camera: Blackmagic Camera, vertical on, 4K, 24 fps, shutter 360° (1/24 s), ISO 400 to start and never past 1600, white balance 3200K tint 0 (2800K for the blue variant), manual focus locked on the lit wall, 1x or 2x, never the ultrawide, stabilization off, zebras at 95% inside the brightest bulb only, Display LUT on and Record LUT to Clip on for CapCut. Brace dead-still: car roof, post, wall or the Osmo locked as a stand. Roll 8 s a take, keep 3–5 s. In November Santa Fe sunset is about 5:00 pm, so shoot 5:30–6:30 pm, clear or thin-cloud nights only.

Framing: subject in the middle third, horizon in the lower third, dark sky above, straight on or a slight angle, far enough back that the whole building fits with air around it. The routine for every setup: plant, frame, expose for the lights with ISO only, check the colour (grey sky means 2800K, cyan means 3600K), focus and lock, hands off and count 8, wait for the car, move on. Ten setups in an hour is a good night. Do not review on the spot; the LUT preview is the review.
